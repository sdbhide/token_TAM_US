01  /  Architecture

One market, two stochastic processes

The market is an additive sum of two compound processes. Bifurcation is not presentational: it is what lets the retail and commercial tiers be calibrated, and go wrong, independently.

TAM = TAMi + TAMe = Ni∑j=1 Xi,j  +  Ne∑k=1 Xe,k Ns = adopting units in segment s  ·  Xs = annual spend per unit

Why a compound sum. Two independent sources of ignorance sit in this market: how many buyers there will be, and how much each spends. A point estimate multiplies two guesses and discards both error terms. A compound sum propagates them, and their interaction is exactly what the third moment picks up.

Why bifurcate. No single distribution holds a $179 consumer mean and a $4,866 enterprise mean with a 129× gap in tail weight without distorting both. Two segments cost four parameters and buy independent calibration, independent levers, and a clean variance attribution.

Assumptions, and where they bind

A1
i.i.d. within segment. Spends are exchangeable given the segment. Heterogeneity lives in the lognormal, not across units.
A2
Counts ⊥ spends. Violated at partial penetration; early adopters skew high-income. Approximately holds at the saturation equilibrium being priced.
A3
Segments independent. Common shocks make this false and the direction is known: independence understates variance. A covariance term is carried explicitly.
A4
Stable prices. TAM = price × volume with price fixed. Jevons-type demand response to falling token prices is out of scope, not assumed away.

The object being summed

Behaviour

The mean of this object is easy and almost uninformative. Everything decision-relevant (tail risk, whether error bars are legal, which parameter to spend calibration effort on) lives in the second and third moments, which is what the rest of the framework solves for.

02  /  Unit spend

Separating capacity to spend from propensity to spend

Per-unit expenditure is modelled as a product of an observable scale factor and an unobservable share. The product of two lognormals is lognormal, so the decomposition is free.

Xi = Ki · Ii      Xe = Ke · Ie

I: the base

Personal income for consumers; IT-budget / firm scale for enterprises. Externally observable, externally calibrated. Carries no opinion about AI.

K: the share

Fraction of that base spent on inference. The genuinely unknown object, and the one every disagreement about this market is actually about.

μX,s = μI,s + μK,s      σ2X,s = σ2I,s + σ2K,s + 2ρsσI,sσK,s Log-scales add; log-dispersions add with a correlation cross-term

Why this closure matters. Because the product stays lognormal, the entire moment machinery downstream (truncation, compound sums, the convergence diagnostic) keeps working on a two-parameter object. The decomposition buys interpretability at literally no analytical cost, which is rare enough to be worth naming.

What ρ does, and why it is the cheapest parameter here

  • Consumers: ρ < 0. Engel-curve behaviour: the token share of income falls as income rises. A negative ρ shrinks σX, thinning the consumer tail.
  • Enterprises: ρ > 0. Agent fleets scale super-linearly with firm size, so large firms spend a larger share of a larger base. This fattens the tail from both ends.
  • ρ = 0 recovers the independent case exactly. One extra parameter per segment, zero loss of tractability, and it enters σ2 linearly but skewness exponentially.

Behaviour

The two segments differ far more in dispersion than in level: σX is 1.9× larger for enterprises, and because dispersion enters the moments through eσ², that gap is what produces every asymmetry downstream.

03  /  Moments

One quantity governs the entire model: eσ²

For a lognormal, location and shape separate cleanly. μ scales the distribution; σ alone decides what it looks like.

E[X] = eμ + σ²/2 Var(X) = (eσ² − 1) e2μ + σ² μ3(X) = (eσ² + 2) √eσ² − 1 · Var(X)3/2

Divide through and the location term vanishes entirely:

CV(X) = √eσ² − 1      γ(X) = (eσ² + 2) √eσ² − 1 Both are functions of σ alone. μ moves the market; σ shapes it.

The same parameter, read as a concentration measure

mean / median = eσ²/2 The wedge between the typical unit and the average unit, the object that reappears as θ vs θmed

  • This is the formal content of "enterprise AI spend is concentrated in a handful of accounts." It is not an anecdote about hyperscalers; it is a single parameter.
  • Every downstream question (convergence, tail risk, whether a normal confidence interval is admissible) therefore reduces to σX,e. The sensitivity analysis rediscovers this numerically; the algebra says it in one line.
  • It also sets the ceiling on precision. Because σX2 = σI2 + σK2 + 2ρσIσK, and σI is externally observable, the only dispersion left to argue about is the penetration rate, which is where calibration effort belongs.
Behaviour

Shape is a one-parameter family. Any intervention that does not move σX,e (more simulation draws, finer population data, a better mean estimate) leaves the risk profile of the answer exactly where it was.

04  /  Truncation

The hard cap is a first-order choice, not a refinement

No firm spends more than its IT budget; no consumer spends more than disposable income. An unbounded tail on a bounded quantity is a modelling error, and it surfaces in the third moment first.

Exact aggregate skewness against enterprise log-dispersion, all else held at calibration. The two curves separate by four orders of magnitude by σ = 3.

E[Xk | X ≤ c] = ekμ + k²σ²/2 · Φ(dk)Φ(d0),    dk = ln c − μ − kσ²σ Raw moments stay closed-form under truncation; central moments follow mechanically
  • A pure shape instrument. A $60M/yr cap moves the enterprise mean from $4,866 to $4,863 (three dollars) while cutting aggregate skewness 0.51 → 0.09. It buys tractability without moving the headline.
  • It voids the asymptotics. Truncation destroys the e3σ² behaviour every approximation ahead rests on. Under a cap only the exact expression applies, which is why the production solver works on exact moments.
  • It makes γ non-monotone in σ. Past a point, extra dispersion no longer builds a tail; it piles mass against the ceiling and the distribution becomes less skewed. The blue curve turns over; the uncapped one never does.
Behaviour

Capping is the highest-leverage, lowest-cost decision in the model: near-zero effect on the estimate, decisive effect on whether the estimate has usable error bars.

05  /  Counts

At national scale, sampling noise is not the uncertainty

The adopting count is not a free input. Deriving it from an adoption probability shows where the variance in this market genuinely comes from, and it is not the coin flips.

Known adoption rate Ns ~ Bin(Pops, ps)  ⇒  Var(Ns) = Pops ps(1 − ps)
Uncertain adoption rate ps ~ Beta(αs, βs)  ⇒  Var(Ns) = Pops p̄s(1 − p̄s) [1 + Pops − 1αs + βs + 1 ] The bracket grows with population, so the second term dominates: Var(N) → Pop² · Var(p)

The orders of magnitude decide it. The binomial term is O(Pop); the parameter-uncertainty term is O(Pop²). At Pop ≈ 108 the second is roughly 108× the first. What we do not know is the adoption rate, not the realisation of the individual draws.

The practical reading. A Beta prior on p is not a technical nicety layered on a binomial; at national scale it is the variance model, and the binomial underneath it is a rounding error.

The crossover, in the calibrated model

Enterprise count-uncertainty threshold
CV(N) > 0.46%
Above this, the count term alone exceeds the entire spend-dispersion contribution to Var(TAMe). Every credible adoption prior is far past it.
  • At a 10% coefficient of variation on adoption, sd(TAM) rises from $0.11B to $3.02B, a 27× widening driven entirely by parameter uncertainty.
  • This is why the deterministic-count approximation used in the convergence analysis must be checked, never assumed. In this calibration it is not merely inaccurate; it is describing a different regime (slide 10).
  • The implementation takes E[N], Var(N) and μ3(N) as inputs, so any count model (Binomial, Beta-Binomial, or a diffusion process supplying three moments) plugs in unchanged.
Behaviour

Var(TAM) is a statement about parameter uncertainty, not sampling error. More simulation draws will never narrow it; a better adoption prior is the only thing that will.

06  /  The engine

Wald, plus the third cumulant

Cumulants of a compound sum compose, KS(t) = KN(ln MX(t)), so the third moment is exact rather than approximate. This is the whole computational engine.

E[TAMs] = E[Ns] E[Xs] Var(TAMs) = E[Ns] Var(Xs)  +  (E[Xs])² Var(Ns) μ3(TAMs) = E[Ns] μ3(Xs)  +  3 Var(Ns) E[Xs] Var(Xs)  +  μ3(Ns) (E[Xs])³

Reading the structure

Every term is one X-moment × one N-moment Spend and count never mix inside a term, which is what makes variance attribution exact and free: no simulation needed to say which parameter owns which share.
The orders differ Spend terms scale as O(E[N]); count terms as O(E[N]²) once Var(N) ∝ Pop². At national scale the second group wins by construction, not by calibration.
Caps drop in without touching this layer Under a hard cap, substitute the truncated moments of X. Correlation, truncation and the count model compose cleanly.

07  /  Aggregation

The scale gap θ, and the covariance we know is missing

Segment aggregation is additive in the first two moments. The interesting object is the ratio between the segments, and it has two definitions, deliberately.

E[TAM] = E[TAMi] + E[TAMe] Var(TAM) = Var(TAMi) + Var(TAMe) + 2 Cov(TAMi, TAMe)

The covariance term is carried, not dropped. The baseline sets it to zero, but both segments respond to the same shocks: capability jumps, price moves, macro conditions. So the sign of the bias is known: independence understates total variance. A known-sign, one-directional bias is the acceptable kind; a latent common factor slots straight into this term without touching anything above it.

θ = E[Xe]E[Xi] = exp[(μX,e − μX,i) + σ2X,e − σ2X,i2 ]

Two θ's, on purpose

Mean ratio θ
27.2
The economically meaningful scale gap. Carries σX,e.
Median ratio θmed

σ-free. Exists only to keep one closed form from being circular.
  • The wedge between them is the tail. A typical enterprise account is about 5× a typical consumer; the average enterprise account is 27×. The 5.5× gap between those two statements is entirely enterprise dispersion.
  • θ is where the closed forms break. Because θ contains σ2X,e, any equation that solves for σX,e with θ on the right-hand side is transcendental, not closed-form. θmed is the honest workaround; the numeric solver is the answer (slide 10).
  • Report both. Quoting a mean ratio as if it described a typical account is the single easiest way to mislead a client about this market.
Behaviour

Mean and median diverge by construction under lognormality, and the divergence is a measurement of concentration rather than a nuisance. The gap between θ and θmed is the cleanest single summary of how top-heavy enterprise demand is.

08  /  Diagnostic

When is a normal confidence interval admissible?

The convergence analysis does not estimate TAM; the simulation does. It answers a narrower and more practical question: may I report ±1.96σ, or must I report simulated quantiles?

Enterprise-dominated regime, ε = 0.1. The threshold rises logarithmically; the calibrated dispersion sits above it at every plausible market size.

γTAM = μ3(TAMi) + μ3(TAMe)(Var(TAMi) + Var(TAMe))3/2  ≤  ε Exact, from the compound moments of slide 7
γ ≈ e(3/2)σ²X,e / √E[Ne]  ⇒  σX,e(crit) = √ ln(ε² E[Ne]) / 3  Real only when ε² E[Ne] > 1
  • It grows like √log N. Six orders of magnitude in market size (103 to 109 accounts) moves the threshold only from 0.88 to 2.32. Loosening ε fourfold, from 0.05 to 0.20, buys just 1.77 to 2.02. Against a heavy tail the central limit theorem arrives logarithmically slowly.
  • The verdict here. σX,e = 2.163 sits above σcrit = 1.899, giving γ = 0.51, five times tolerance. Analytical intervals are inadmissible; report simulated quantiles.
Behaviour

The threshold moves like the square root of a logarithm while the skewness it is fighting moves like e3σ²/2. Concentration, not market size, decides whether this market is analytically predictable, and the value of the diagnostic is precisely that it can fail. A check that always passes says nothing about when to trust your own error bars.

09  /  Regimes

Three regimes, and the discipline of knowing which one you are in

Each closed form below is correct inside its own envelope and badly wrong outside it. The envelopes are checkable, which is what makes the approximations usable at all.

A · Enterprise-dominated

Holds when E[Ne]eσ²X,eθ² ≫ E[Ni](eσ²X,i−1).

σcrit = √ln(ε²E[Ne])/3

Here: this is the regime. The closed form returns 1.899 against the exact numeric solution 1.894, agreement to 0.3%.

Note what the threshold does not contain: no θ, no consumer parameter, no μ. In this regime the entire question reduces to enterprise dispersion against enterprise account count.

B · Consumer-anchored

The mirror condition, with the consumer variance in the denominator.

σcrit = √ ln(ε²E[Ni]³(eσ²X,i−1)³E[Ne]² θmed6)/6 

The θmed6 denominator is decisive. For θ ≳ 10² the log-argument falls below 1 and no real root exists. A domain failure is the formula telling you to use Regime A.

C · Count-dominated

The regime both approximations discard, and the one the calibration is actually in, once adoption uncertainty is admitted (slide 6).

γTAM → γp · H,   H = ∑s TAMs³(∑s TAMs²)3/2

The spend tail cancels out of γ entirely. Aggregate skewness is inherited from the adoption prior, scaled by a pure segment-share factor H ∈ [1/√2, 1]. Here H = 0.731, matching the exact moments to four digits.

The transcendental problem

θ contains σX,e, so solving for σcrit with θ on the right-hand side is a transcendental equation wearing the clothes of a closed form. Either substitute the σ-free θmed, or root-find on the exact γ: Brent's method, regime-free, cap- and ρ-aware, hitting γ = ε to ~10−9.

Why keep the closed forms at all

Because the solver returns a number and the algebra returns a reason. σcrit ∝ √log N is visible in one line and invisible in any amount of output. The closed forms say which lever matters; the solver says what the answer is. Reporting the first as the second is the error this derivation is built to catch.

Behaviour

The model has a regime structure, and the regime (not the parameter values) determines which formula is even meaningful. Checking the envelope before quoting a result is the whole methodological point.

A1  /  Derivations

Two results quoted without proof in the main deck

Truncated central moments

Raw moments under an upper truncation at c follow from completing the square in the lognormal integral, giving the ratio of normal CDFs on slide 5. Central moments then follow mechanically:

Var = m2 − m12 μ3 = m3 − 3m1m2 + 2m13

Both are validated against numerical quadrature to a relative error of about 10−11, and the compound-sum moments against brute-force simulation.

Feasibility bound on the root search

With σK ≥ 0, the smallest attainable dispersion is σI√1−ρ² for ρ < 0, and σI otherwise. That bound is what brackets the numeric solver from below; without it the root-find can wander into parameterisations the model cannot represent.

The count-dominated limit

When Var(N) dominates, each segment total is approximately TAMs ≈ E[Xs] Ns, a deterministic rescaling of the count. If both segments inherit the same adoption-prior skewness γp and coefficient of variation, the E[Xs] and CV factors cancel between numerator and denominator:

γTAM → γp · ∑s as³(∑s as²)3/2,    as = E[TAMs]

The factor is a concentration index on segment shares: it equals 1/√2 ≈ 0.707 for two equal segments and approaches 1 as one segment takes over. At the current 42 / 58 split it is 0.731.

A2  /  Corrections

What re-deriving the framework actually caught

Three errors survived into a prior version of this framework. Each was found by checking the algebra against the code, not by inspection, which is the argument for keeping both.

1 · A dimensional error in the consumer-anchored threshold

The earlier form read ½ ln(·) with no outer square root: wrong coefficient (½ rather than ⅙) and wrong dimension: it returns a σ²-like quantity while being labelled σ. In a test regime where both forms produce a number, the old one overstates σcrit by roughly 7×, which would license normal confidence intervals in a regime that badly fails them. A units check on the output would have caught it before any numerical test did.

2 · A dropped factor in the regime condition

The enterprise-dominance condition was stated as E[Ne]θ² ≫ E[Ni]eσ²X,i, omitting the eσ²X,e factor. That factor is negligible in the benign case and dominant in the high-concentration case; that is, it matters precisely in the regime the analysis exists to study.

3 · A "closed form" that was transcendental

Because θ carries σX,e, solving for σcrit with θ on the right-hand side is not a closed form. The fix is stated rather than hidden: use the σ-free θmed and accept the reinterpretation, or root-find on the exact expression. Both are implemented; the second is the one that gets reported.

The pattern. None of these change the headline TAM. All three change whether the reported uncertainty around it is defensible, which is the part a client, a journalist, or a referee will actually lean on.
