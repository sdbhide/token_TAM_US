1 · Model architecture

One TAM, two economically distinct stochastic processes

Consumers and enterprises differ in population scale, spend scale, and tail concentration. Modeling them separately preserves those structural differences before aggregation.

Structural question: the same mean TAM can arise from very different combinations of adoption, spend dispersion, and concentration, so the distribution matters, not only the point estimate.

3 · Closed-form comparative statics

The mean is simple; dispersion is nonlinear

The lognormal moments make parameter behavior transparent before any simulation is run.

Level
E[X] = eμ + σ²/2

μ shifts the entire distribution one-for-one in logs. σ also raises the mean through Jensen’s inequality.

Variance
Var(X)
(eσ² − 1)e2μ+σ²

Dispersion is not “just uncertainty.” In a lognormal, more dispersion also reallocates mass into very high-spend outcomes.

Skewness driver
μ₃(X)
∝ (eσ²+2)√(eσ²−1) · Var(X)3/2

The third moment accelerates fastest. That is why enterprise concentration can dominate predictability even when the mean remains plausible.

↑ μI or μKRaises mean spend proportionally in logs; all dollar moments scale upward.
↑ σI, σK, or ρRaises σ²X; mean rises, but variance and skewness rise much faster.
ImplicationCalibrating dispersion is economically consequential, not merely a confidence-band exercise.

4 · Tail discipline

A hard cap is a modeling choice, not a cosmetic refinement

The unconstrained lognormal has infinite support. Economic budgets do not. Truncation preserves closed-form moments while removing implausible tail mass.

Truncated raw moments remain analytic
E[Xk | X≤c] = ekμ + k²σ²/2 · Φ(dk)/Φ(d0)
dk = (ln c − μ − kσ²)/σ
Lower capExpected behavior
E[X] ↓Moderate effect unless the cap cuts into meaningful probability mass.
Var(X) ↓↓Large high-spend realizations lose disproportionate weight.
μ₃(X) ↓↓↓Third moments are most tail-sensitive; aggregate skewness can collapse quickly.
Interpretation: the cap encodes an economic feasibility constraint. If it materially changes Gaussianity, that is information about how much the uncapped result relied on implausible extremes.

5 · Adoption counts

At national scale, uncertainty about p can matter more than sampling noise

The number of adopters is itself random. The distinction between a known adoption rate and an uncertain adoption rate changes the order of magnitude of count variance.

Known adoption probability

Binomial count

N ~ Binomial(Pop, p)
E[N] = Pop·p
Var(N) = Pop·p(1−p)
Scaling: sampling variance is O(Pop).
Uncertain adoption probability

Beta-Binomial count

p ~ Beta(α,β) ⇒ N ~ Beta-Binomial
Var(N) = Pop·p̄(1−p̄) [1 + (Pop−1)/(α+β+1)]
Scaling: parameter uncertainty contributes an O(Pop²) term.
US-scale implication: “millions of adopters” does not automatically make N deterministic. If the equilibrium adoption rate is uncertain, uncertainty in p can dominate the realized Bernoulli noise.

6 · Compound-sum closed forms

The formulas identify exactly where aggregate uncertainty comes from

For TAMs = Σj=1…Nₛ Xs,j, the first three moments separate spend heterogeneity, adoption uncertainty, and their interaction.

E[TAMs] = E[Ns] · E[Xs]
Var(TAMs) = E[Ns] Var(Xs) + E[Xs]2 Var(Ns)
Spend-risk term
E[N] · Var(X)

More adopters diversify idiosyncratic spend, but aggregate variance still accumulates linearly with N.

Adoption-risk term
E[X]² · Var(N)

Uncertainty in how many units adopt is magnified by the square of average spend.

Third-moment interaction
3 Var(N) E[X] Var(X)

Count uncertainty and spend dispersion interact directly in aggregate skewness.

μ₃(TAMs) = E[N]μ₃(X) + 3Var(N)E[X]Var(X) + μ₃(N)E[X]³

7 · Whole-market structure

Scale gaps affect the level; common shocks affect the risk

Aggregation is additive, but two structural objects deserve separate attention: the enterprise-to-consumer scale gap θ, and cross-segment covariance.

Structural scale factor
θ = E[Xₑ] / E[Xᵢ]
θ = exp[(μX,e−μX,i) + (σ²X,e−σ²X,i)/2]
1
Mean-ratio θ is economically meaningful, but it inherits enterprise dispersion.
2
Median-ratio θmed removes σX,e and is useful only to keep one convergence approximation algebraically closed.
Aggregation with a covariance hook
E[TAM] = E[TAMᵢ] + E[TAMₑ]
Var(TAM) = Var(TAMᵢ) + Var(TAMₑ) + 2Cov(TAMᵢ,TAMₑ)

Baseline: Cov = 0 for tractability.

Economic reality: model capability, token prices, and macro conditions can move both segments together.

Direction of bias: positive common shocks mean the independent-segment baseline understates total variance.

8 · Gaussian convergence diagnostic

Large N helps, but concentration can overwhelm diversification

The exact diagnostic is aggregate skewness. Closed-form approximations then reveal which structural parameters govern the speed of convergence toward normality.

Exact criterion
γTAM = [μ₃(TAMᵢ)+μ₃(TAMₑ)] / [Var(TAMᵢ)+Var(TAMₑ)]3/2 ≤ ε
Regime A · enterprise-dominated variance
γ ≈ e(3/2)σ²X,e / √E[Nₑ]
σcrit = √{ (1/3) ln[ε² E[Nₑ]] }
Parameter moveEffect on Gaussianity
↑ NₑHelps, but only through √log(Nₑ) in σcrit.
↑ σX,eHurts explosively: log γ rises as 1.5σ².
↑ θApproximately cancels in this regime.
Schematic convergence frontier
Regime B: when consumer variance anchors the denominator, θ enters with very high power. With realistic θ ≫ 1, the domain condition often fails, signaling that Regime A or the exact numeric solver is the appropriate description.

B · Assumptions & extensions

Where the baseline can fail, and the direction of concern

A1
Within-segment i.i.d. spend abstracts from network effects and account-level clustering.
A2
N ⟂ X. At partial penetration, adopters may be positively selected on income, firm size, or AI intensity.
A3
Consumer ⟂ enterprise. Common macro/capability shocks imply positive covariance, so the baseline can understate total variance.
A4
Stable token prices. Price decline and demand elasticity can create Jevons-style feedback between price and token volume.
Most important structural extensions
  • Latent common factor: model cross-segment covariance explicitly.
  • Pareto-spliced enterprise tail: test whether lognormal understates extreme account concentration.
  • Dynamic diffusion: separate equilibrium adoption from the path to equilibrium.
  • Selection: allow early adopters to have systematically different X distributions.
  • Price endogeneity: separate token-volume TAM from dollar TAM when prices move.
Diagnostic principle: when a proposed extension breaks a finite third moment or invalidates N/E[N] → 1, the Gaussian convergence machinery should fail visibly rather than silently.
